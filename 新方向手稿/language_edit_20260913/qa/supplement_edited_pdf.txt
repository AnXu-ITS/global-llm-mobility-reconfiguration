S3
Statistical and outcome conventions
S3.1
Fixed-panel estimand and random streams
The runner initializes a fresh random.Random(seed) for every scenario and visits aircraft in sorted order.
Each aircraft consumes an endurance draw, and each busy aircraft consumes an additional progress draw.
SUMO also reuses the same seed. Workload-dependent draw counts change the realizations, but they do
not create independently keyed scenario streams. We therefore retain dependence within each seed across
the fixed scenario panel and pair policies by scenario and seed. This decision follows the implementation,
rather than seed labels or state hashes alone.
For a complete panel, each seed contributes the average of its scenario-specific outcomes or paired
differences. Means keep equal scenario–run weights. A t interval uses the standard deviation across 20
seed averages (10 in the E1 ablation). For a conditional endpoint with N retained pairs, let ¯d denote
the original pair-weighted mean and K the number of represented seeds. The cluster contribution is

S3.2
Service-loss audit
All 2,880 E3 run registries were checked against their 900 s horizons. At each site, 1,440 task instances
had deadlines at or before the horizon, and all were COMPLETED. Across sites, zero overdue tasks
remained EN_ROUTE or ASSIGNED. This dataset therefore requires no SWL correction or sensitivity
analysis for overdue-task penalties. Background missions with 1,800 s deadlines are not classified as
failures solely because they remain in service at 900 s. Incumbent damage remains a count of damaged
final states.
As a complementary E1 measure, we linked recorded preemption times to the incumbent identified
in the candidate log and its final interrupted state. At A, exposure from preemption to the horizon
for persistently interrupted services averages 35 s per run for B2 and 80 s for B4b (B1: 300 s). This
measure is truncated at the horizon; it does not capture the complete cumulative cost of interruption.
Mission CSV records omit some incumbent preemption transitions, so a logged interruption duration
of zero would be misleading. Initial incumbent deadlines are 1,800 s, beyond the 900 s horizon. This
exposure measure therefore supports no claim about incumbent lateness.
S4
Technical checks and timing sensitivity
S4.1
Duplicate pending commands and E4B regression
The original B0 D60 pipeline records a GROUND_FALLBACK command at 300 s, due at 360 s. At 360
s, the fault triggers a new decision with the same normalized action and the same 181.763 s ETA field.
The original queue replaces the pending command and restarts the 60 s wait. It issues the command
at 420 s, and transport completes at 602 s. This extra wait produced the original 302 s completion
duration.
The revised queue keeps the original due time when a validated normalized action exactly matches a
pending command. A changed action still supersedes the pending command, and the surviving command
is revalidated at execution. The queue_fix.py overlay implements this correction without changing the
original project source or raw runs.
All 20 B0 D60 seeds were rerun through SUMO–BlueSky.
The command issued at 360 s, and
transport completed at 542 s, giving a duration of 242 s. Single-seed D00/D30 controls retain 182/212
s; fault times 359/361 s both give 242 s. B2’s changed air-to-ground command retains a 302 s duration.
B1 retains its air-to-air replacement sequence. Patched and unpatched control reruns both give 342 s
8

in the current runtime, compared with the archived 341 s. This 1 s reproduction difference is unrelated
to deduplication.
We retain the archived cell because the correction does not change the command
sequence. These regressions made no LLM calls. The original B4b traces contain genuine command
replacement and remain unchanged.

S4.2
Physical transfer semantics and offline added-time sensitivity
The model replaces service assignments without tracking individual payloads. New air candidates route
through the mission origin. Interrupted-service candidates route through V3 before the destination, but
the supervisor can dispatch a backup before the original carrier reaches V3. Preemption interrupts and
detaches the incumbent mission. Ground takeover starts a service timer based on the current reference
corridor ETA. The model has no explicit states for unloading, rendezvous, loading, new inventory or
re-shipment.
We evaluate added transfer time through an offline trajectory-fixed sensitivity, rather than a new
closed-loop experiment. For an actually affected E2 mission with a post-failure DISPATCH, REASSIGN
or GROUND_FALLBACK, we add one hypothesized transfer delay h to recorded completion.
The
rule is the same for every policy; uninterrupted services receive no added time. A previously timely
completion becomes late when 0 ≤dm −cm < h. Choices, fault exposure and later scheduling remain
fixed. The values h = 15, 30, 60 s are illustrative assumptions, not measured handling durations.

S4.3
Road-statistics boundary
The original morphology routine summed all imported passenger edges and divided by 10.24 km2, without
clipping them to its bbox argument.
The revised calculation intersects each edge polyline with the
configured geographic rectangle after projection through the SUMO network transform. It apportions
SUMO edge lengths by the fraction of the polyline inside the rectangle and uses the same polygon’s area
for density. Intersection counts include only nodes inside the polygon, but count their distinct neighbors
in the full graph to avoid artificial boundary intersections. Original full-network topology, OD samples
and routing remain separately identified and unchanged. Site C still has higher intersection density than
A; its description instead draws on dead ends, degree, circuity and facility distance.
